# PIT BT–BU: local billing foundation (not a launch approval)

## Product contract and status

All 178 existing task experiences remain free. Basic Explorer reads stay subject only to technical limits. No paid operation is enabled or connected to either static site. The following are **provisional local-test fixtures**, not public prices: Pro €9.90/month and 100 credits per paid month; packs €5/50 credits, €10/110 credits, and €25/300 credits. Credits are non-transferable internal service units, not tokens, stored value, or a cash equivalent. Anonymous x402 payment, if ever implemented, authorizes only its exact paid operation and does not mint account credits.

Future candidate premium operations are batch reports (10 credits), AI analysis (25), and enhanced exports (3). They are deliberately disabled in `billing/catalog.mjs`. There is no paywall, checkout, AI operation, premium export, or paid resource in the product. A chargeable operation must have measured per-run compute/provider cost, validated customer utility and legal review before its `enabled` flag changes.
`billing/economics.mjs` calculates exact micro-euro net receipts after stated processor assumptions and returns `INCOMPLETE` until actual marginal and allocated-overhead costs are supplied. Even a positive modeled margin leaves the operation disabled.

The draft subscription policy is access through the paid period, cancellation at period end, renewal credits only after verified payment, and fail-closed access on payment failure or expiration. Existing purchased or granted credits currently carry forward until spent; no automatic credit expiry exists. A full refund removes unused credits, freezes the account for review, and records any already-spent shortfall as an unrecovered internal liability, without making the balance negative. Credits applied after a deficit first reduce that liability. This is an implementation policy for local tests, **not** a consumer-rights determination. The operator must approve expiry, refund, cooling-off/withdrawal, tax/VAT, chargeback, and disclosure terms with qualified counsel before real money.

## Cost screen, not price validation

As one illustrative merchant-country assumption (the operator's country is unknown), the published [Stripe France standard EEA-card rate](https://stripe.com/fr/pricing) is 1.5% + €0.25. Processor cost would be approximately €0.325/€0.40/€0.625 for the €5/€10/€25 packs, and €0.3985 for €9.90 Pro. Ignoring tax, disputes, conversion, subscription features and infrastructure, net receipts per credit would be about €0.0935, €0.08727, €0.08125 and €0.09502 respectively. [CoinGate reports a standard merchant fee of 1%](https://support.coingate.com/hc/en-us/articles/4402499595794-What-fees-do-I-have-to-pay-when-I-use-CoinGate-services), with possible conversion and refund costs. The €25 pack gives the lowest indicative net receipt per credit. Any proposed service must have measured marginal cost comfortably below that floor after allocating overhead and tax; this has **not** been demonstrated. No price or credit quantity is approved for publication.

Cloudflare [D1 uses SQLite semantics and local development](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/local-development/). As documented in October 2026, Workers Free is limited to [100,000 requests/day and 10 ms CPU/request](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/); D1 Free includes [5 million rows read/day, 100,000 rows written/day and 5 GB storage](https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/). D1 can return errors after free limits are reached. These limits are not a production capacity guarantee, and paid providers and operational support introduce costs even at low traffic. No account, D1 instance, Worker or paid service was created in this phase.

## Implemented local components

- `billing/schema.sql`: D1-compatible `STRICT` SQLite schema for users, wallet balances, orders, subscriptions, audit ledger, provider events, reversals and one-time paid-operation receipts. Unique source/event/payment keys and nonnegative balance constraints are enforced in SQL.
- `billing/store.mjs`: synchronous local `node:sqlite` fixture using `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transactions. Purchase, debit, refund and subscription state changes commit atomically. Repeated provider events do not issue duplicate credits. Negative balances are forbidden. Time is explicit. This is a local fixture, not a deployed D1/Worker adapter.
- Gross credit grants and subsequent liability recoveries appear as separate immutable ledger entries, so net ledger deltas reconcile to the wallet balance. A refund that exceeds the available balance is recorded in `reversals` and freezes the account; it never creates a negative wallet balance.
- `billing/local-api.mjs`: in-process fixture identity mapping. No HTTP listener, public test token, mock-auth Worker or deployable API is exported. Cross-user balance access is not exposed through the facade. A real session/auth provider and authorization review are prerequisites to any network API.
- `billing/payments.mjs`: test-mode Stripe webhook raw-body HMAC/timestamp verification with order association; a CoinGate-style **mock trusted order readback** enforcing chain/token/recipient/amount/expiry; and a mock x402 v2 challenge/verify/settle boundary. None can move funds. The CoinGate mock is not a live callback verifier; production would need callback-token verification plus an authenticated provider order readback. The x402 mock is not a signature implementation or real settlement facilitator.
- `pnpm billing:test` and `pnpm billing:demo` run entirely locally with no network or credentials. `node:sqlite` is experimental in the pinned Node 22 runtime; a production D1 adapter would require its own integration and concurrency qualification.

The [Stripe webhook guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) requires verifying the raw payload and treating deliveries as retryable/unordered. [CoinGate callbacks](https://developer.coingate.com/reference/payment-callback) include order status and callback token; a callback alone is not sufficient settlement evidence for this architecture. The [x402 v2 specification](https://github.com/x402-foundation/x402/blob/main/specs/x402-specification-v2.md) defines `PAYMENT-REQUIRED`, `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE` and `PAYMENT-RESPONSE`, with facilitator verification and settlement. The mock follows these boundaries without claiming a compatible real payment signature. The proposed address `0x8B87aECC9413C2c5b853e81AA94b60990e7aBdbE` remains **UNVERIFIED**, appears in no runtime configuration, and must not receive funds without ownership/payout verification.

## Security and launch boundary

No client payment-success flag is accepted by the local API. Paid events require provider verification in the adapter; the store is an internal trust boundary and must never be directly exposed as HTTP. Missing/invalid signatures, mismatched orders, unpaid events, failed settlement, duplicate payment IDs, insufficient balance and database exceptions fail closed. The local x402 test double may only be used in tests and is not exported into a public Worker. Existing trading flags remain false; payment credits are unrelated to PIT trading fees.

Before a real-money launch: select actual premium operations and measure cost; settle credit expiry and consumer-rights policy; establish legal publisher, privacy, VAT and customer-support details; obtain real Stripe/CoinGate accounts and test credentials; verify the receiving wallet; implement production auth, hosted D1 adapter, official reviewed x402 v2 facilitator/SDK path, provider API-version mappings, reconciliation and operational monitoring; run sandbox end-to-end tests and a security review. None of these are complete.

## Validation

`pnpm billing:test` covers signed and rejected payments, idempotency, out-of-order refunds, cancellation, renewal, concurrency across independent SQLite connections, insufficient funds, cross-user facade isolation, crypto settlement mismatches and x402 verify/settle failures. `pnpm billing:demo` shows one simulated purchase and debit. Existing Toolbox, Explorer and six-tool beta static packages are validated separately; no billing file is included in their source trees or route allowlists. Record the exact package hashes and Git state in the phase report after final checks.
