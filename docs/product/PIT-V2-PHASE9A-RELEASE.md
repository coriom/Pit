## Payment security and accounting

The two routers bind immutable payment token and fee recipient. Their owner is the dedicated PIT test signer for the proposed testnet trial and can change prices; each change increments `priceVersion`. A quote binds an intent ID, version, maximum fee and deadline. The contract rejects zero/replayed intents, expired quotes and a fee above the reviewed maximum. `SafeERC20` moves the exact six-decimal amount from the caller directly to the recipient. Payer and recipient balance deltas must match; fee transfer, operation and intent use are atomic. MultiSender verifies exact standard-token balances and refuses unsupported fee-on-transfer/rebasing behavior. The creator receives advanced token administration; the routers and PIT do not receive hidden token roles. Claims on existing lockers/vesting contracts and free read tools remain free.

`src/operations/lib/payment-reconciliation.ts` requires a successful receipt in the expected canonical block, the expected destination, and one matching fee event, operation event and test-token transfer with separate log indices. It supports a configurable confirmation threshold and deduplicates `(chain, transaction, log index)` while rejecting conflicting block/intent evidence. Local JSON receipt v2 includes block hash, fee, event key and confirmation count. `PREPARED` means a quote only; `SUBMITTED` means a hash with an uncertain outcome; `MINED` is a receipt awaiting the confirmation policy; `CONFIRMED` requires canonical event and state readback. `REVERTED`, `REORGED` and `MISMATCH` are distinct; a timeout is not a failed payment. The local browser demo does not provide a durable merchant ledger or legal accounting system. Before commerce, a backend/indexer must reconcile confirmations and reorganizations, archive original receipt evidence, enforce retention policy, and support refund and tax records without requiring permanent customer profiles.

There is no emergency pause. Client routing can be disabled, but direct contract calls remain possible. An immutable wrong recipient requires replacement contracts. The price owner is powerful and must be an operator-controlled address with monitored changes. A smart-contract review by an independent auditor, payment operations review, and recipient ownership/business verification remain mandatory before real-value use. This internal review found no unresolved critical/high issue in the local-only flows; it is **not an independent audit**.

Each contains only reviewed Solidity/OpenZeppelin source, standard JSON compiler settings, contract identifier and existing creation transaction hash. All six current Sourcify v2 read-only lookups returned HTTP 404 at preflight. Source publication requires `GO PIT9A SOURCIFY` for the exact payload hashes; no POST was made. The two proposed routers cannot be source-verified before they exist, and require a later reviewed payload/approval if deployed.

## Legal and operational review fields

**Draft only; no commercial offer is approved.** Terms require the actual legal operator identity, jurisdiction, supported service description, user responsibilities, contract-risk limits, support contact and dispute process. Privacy notice requires controller identity, public RPC recipients, browser-local datasets, optional transaction evidence retention, purposes, lawful bases and deletion/retention schedule. Risk notice must explain irreversible blockchain actions, approval scope, token/contract risk, gas loss on revert and no guarantee of public RPC availability. Payment/refund terms must distinguish atomic on-chain fee rollback from gas costs and separately settled digital services, with explicit eligibility and contact process. Accounting needs event/receipt archives, chain reorganization policy, invoice/tax treatment, refunds and merchant identity records. Professional legal, regulatory and tax review must decide the applicable obligations; no PSCA status or exemption is asserted.
