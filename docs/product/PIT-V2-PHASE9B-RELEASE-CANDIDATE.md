## Auditor package: assets, permissions, threats

```
Circle test USDC --fee transfer--> immutable test recipient
payer --paid creator--> newly deployed ERC-20, initial supply to selected recipient
payer --paid operations--> ERC-20 MultiSender / new funded Locker / new funded Vesting
payer --approval--> exact router allowance (historical residual allowance = 0)
```

`PitPaidBase` holds no USDC after a successful operation; it uses SafeERC20 and exact payer/recipient balance deltas, one-use intent IDs per payer/router, quote deadlines, fee maxima, versioned prices and a reentrancy guard. The price owner can update prices, which increments the version and invalidates old quotes; it cannot change the immutable payment token or fee recipient. The routers do not own user-created tokens or receive their admin roles. The advanced ERC-20 default administrator is the creator, with a one-day transfer delay; mint/pause roles follow the preset. MultiSender takes assets from the caller's allowance and distributes atomically. Locker and Vesting **do hold user tokens** under immutable beneficiaries and time rules. NFT collection owners/authorized holders retain the permissions set in their respective contracts; NFT operator approvals can be collection-wide.

Primary threats for external review: misbound fee recipient or payment token, malicious/nonstandard asset tokens, fee-on-transfer accounting, incorrect user roles, reentrancy through token or NFT callbacks, allowance scope, replay/double charge, ambiguous pending receipts, chain reorganization, RPC or UI spoofing, delayed release accounting, and disclosure of private financial inputs. Important invariants are exact fee conservation, no fee after a reverted operation, no intent reuse within a router, no unauthorized mint/transfer, no cap overflow, no partial batch, and no hidden PIT claim authority. Foundry unit/fuzz/stateful invariant suites and local Anvil receipts provide internal evidence only.

Residual design limits: immutable fee recipients require a replacement deployment if the recipient changes; Circle's test-USDC implementation is external; direct permissionless contract use cannot be made fee-enforced; nonstandard tokens are rejected or constrained, not universally supported. An independent security audit, merchant ownership proof, legal/commercial identity, terms, privacy, payment/refund policy, tax/accounting retention and regulatory qualification are still required before real-value charges or mainnet. No PSCA status is claimed. The historical SIWE Worker and its dirty Phase CE source remain outside this accountless payment path.
